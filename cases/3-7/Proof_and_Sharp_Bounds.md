# Grassmann convexity for (3,7): a computer-assisted certificate

**Status: candidate proof, not independently peer reviewed.** The exact
finite certificate described here passes several independent mathematical
and software checks. It proves the stronger pointwise bound below provided
the stated finite-state coverage and generic-perturbation arguments and the
verification programs are correct. It is not an elementary proof or a
formally verified proof-assistant development.

Let $M=(v_1,\ldots,v_7)$ be a real $3\times7$ matrix whose maximal
minors are nonzero, and write
$$
p_{ijk}=\det(v_i,v_j,v_k).
$$
An elementary positive move is
$v_j\mapsto v_j+t v_{j+1}$, $1\le j\le6$, with $t>0$. Let
$r(M)$ be the supremum, over finite sequences of such moves, of the
number of sign changes of $p_{123}$. Define
$$
m(M)=\min_{\omega:\ \omega(v_i)\ne0\ \forall i}
 \operatorname{var}\bigl(\omega(v_1),\ldots,\omega(v_7)\bigr),
$$
where variation counts adjacent sign changes.

The certificate gives
$$
\boxed{r(M)\le 8+m(M).}\tag{1}
$$
There is a parity refinement. After normalizing $p_{567}>0$, put
$\epsilon=1$ if $p_{123}<0$ and $\epsilon=0$ otherwise. The
maximum $r(M)$ has parity $\epsilon$, so
$$
r(M)\le 8+m(M)-\bigl((8+m(M)-\epsilon)\bmod2\bigr).
$$
The parity statement follows from the terminal-state theorem in Section 6:
a maximizing trajectory cannot end with a negative target, since it could
then be extended by another target change.
There are exact examples attaining $8+m$ for every $m=0,1,2,3,4$.
Since $m(M)\le4$, this yields the desired equality
$$
\boxed{r(3,7)=12.}
$$
In fact, for every generic starting matrix,
$$
\boxed{r(M)=12\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m(M)=4.}
$$
The forward implication follows from the computed bound; the reverse
implication has the constructive positive-cell proof in Section 5. Every
such start admits a 12-crossing sequence of at most 40 positive moves.
The upper bound is computer-assisted. The published equivalence between
the discrete and continuous formulations is Lemma 2 in Saldanha, Shapiro,
and Shapiro, [*Finiteness of rank for Grassmann convexity*](https://doi.org/10.5802/crmath.343).
The same paper records the general
lower bound $k(n-k)$; the explicit rational example below also supplies
the lower bound directly.

## 1. The finite state space

The move preserves $p_{567}$. A left change of basis lets us assume
$p_{567}>0$ without changing the number of target sign changes or the
row space. For each generic matrix record the signs of:

* the 35 minors $p_{ijk}$;
* the 15 expressions
  $$
  F_{ij,kl}^{67}=p_{ij6}p_{kl7}-p_{ij7}p_{kl6},
  $$
  where $i,j,k,l$ are four distinct members of $\{1,\ldots,5\}$
  and each of the three pairings of each four-set is used; and
* the additional expressions
  $$
  G_{56}=p_{125}p_{346}-p_{126}p_{345},\qquad
  G_{57}=p_{125}p_{347}-p_{127}p_{345}.
  $$

Thus a strict state has 52 signs. The first 50 signs are generated by a
finite model containing every generic real state, but possibly also
nonrealizable states. It has 16,051,200 base states. The G-sign filter
below leaves 23,246,848 augmented states.

Geometrically, if $L_{12}=v_1\times v_2$ and
$L_{34}=v_3\times v_4$, then
$G_{ij}=(L_{12}\times L_{34})\cdot(v_i\times v_j)$. Thus
$G_{ij}=0$ records the projective incidence in which line $ij$
passes through the intersection of lines $12$ and $34$. The two new
signs retain information about this incidence that the original 50-sign
model omitted.

Here is the coverage argument. After a positive-determinant change of
basis take $v_6=(0,1,0)$, $v_7=(0,0,1)$, and
$v_i=x_i(1,y_i,z_i)$ for $1\le i\le5$. Then $x_5>0$. Put
$P_i=(y_i,z_i)$, $e_{ij}=P_j-P_i$, and let $a=(1,0)$,
$b=(0,1)$. Direct determinant identities give
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{ijk}&=x_i x_j x_k\det(e_{ij},e_{ik}),\\
p_{ij6}&=x_i x_j\det(e_{ij},a),\qquad
p_{ij7}=x_i x_j\det(e_{ij},b),\\
F_{ij,kl}^{67}&=x_i x_j x_k x_l\det(e_{ij},e_{kl}).
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
$$
The other minors $p_{i67}$ have signs $\operatorname{sgn}x_i$.
After temporarily reorienting columns 1–4 so all $x_i>0$, all 50 signs
are therefore determined by the circular order of the ten directed edge
vectors and the two coordinate axes, together with their opposites. The
16 possible original signs of $x_1,\ldots,x_4$ are restored at the end.

For the ten edge directions, the 15 disjoint-edge bracket signs satisfy
16 explicit linear bracket identities. The full rank-two
Grassmann–Plücker sign relations are also necessary for their 45 pairwise
orientation signs. An independent enumeration of the resulting necessary
conditions produces exactly 4,560 ten-direction types. For each type the
first axis occupies one of 20 gaps, the second one of 11 allowed gaps,
and there are 16 reorientations. Consequently the independent key
reconstruction gives
$$
4560\cdot20\cdot11\cdot16=16{,}051{,}200
$$
distinct keys, exactly the keys used by the rank verifier. The 16
identities are checked symbolically; all 210 rank-two four-index
Grassmann–Plücker sign constraints are checked during the independent
enumeration. Only necessity is used: the proof does **not** require every
enumerated state to be realizable.

For the two new signs, write $A_i=p_{12i}$, $B_i=p_{34i}$ and
$G_{ij}=A_iB_j-A_jB_i$, with $G_{67}=F_{12,34}^{67}$. The G-sign
filter uses only necessary sign consequences of the definitions, the
identities
$$
A_5G_{67}-A_6G_{57}+A_7G_{56}=0,\qquad
B_5G_{67}-B_6G_{57}+B_7G_{56}=0,\tag{3}
$$
and the cyclic equality of the three bracket expressions for each G.
In particular, a difference of products has a forced sign when its two
summands have opposite signs. The exact checks of (2), (3), the cyclic
identities, and all filter masks are included in the package.

## 2. The transitions

All 52 tracked polynomials are affine along a single positive elementary
move. Each allowed graph transition flips one sign whose derivative points
toward zero, provided the resulting state passes the necessary sign
filters. Other signs stay fixed. This deliberately over-approximates the
possible real transitions.

For example, the only nonzero derivatives of the two new features are
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &j=2&j=4&j=6\\\hline
G_{56}&p_{134}p_{356}&p_{125}p_{356}&G_{57}
\end{array}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &j=2&j=4&j=5\\\hline
G_{57}&p_{134}p_{357}&p_{125}p_{357}&G_{67}.
\end{array}\tag{4}
$$
The original 108 active feature/move derivatives, the six derivatives in
(4), all inactive cases, and affineness are independently checked by
symbolic substitution or differentiation. In the full model there are
209,042,944 candidate base-feature transitions and 21,586,944 candidate
G-feature transitions, counted with their move labels.

For a finite trajectory, perturb the initial matrix arbitrarily slightly
so that every tracked feature is nonzero at each move endpoint and no
two active features vanish at the same time within a move. This is a
generic condition: for each of the 1,070 pairs of active features the
affine-root resultant is a nonzero polynomial. The package verifies all
1,070 at the single integer witness with columns
$$
(12,-5,1),\ (-7,4,-12),\ (6,9,7),\ (2,-11,-5),\
(6,-11,-11),\ (-3,6,9),\ (-6,12,-4).
$$
It also checks that all 52 feature values at that witness are nonzero.
For a fixed prefix of positive moves, the map from the initial matrix to
the prefix matrix is invertible, so avoiding these finitely many proper
algebraic sets is possible simultaneously. Perturbation preserves all
chosen strict endpoint target signs. It also preserves the starting minor
signs, and therefore preserves $m(M)$. Each event of the perturbed
trajectory is an edge of the over-approximating graph.

## 3. Computing minimum variation from signs

For $a<b<c<d$, the coefficients of the unique dependence of those four
columns have signs
$$
\bigl(p_{bcd},-p_{acd},p_{abd},-p_{abc}\bigr).
$$
For any proposed strict row sign pattern, reorient the columns by that
pattern. Gordan's alternative and Carathéodory's theorem say the pattern
is attained by a linear functional if and only if no four-column circuit
of the reoriented columns has all its coefficients of the same sign.
Generality of the maximal minors rules out smaller circuits. Simultaneous
reversal of all seven signs does not change variation, so just 64 patterns
need be tested. This computes $m(M)$ from the 35 minor signs.

Independently, for generic $M$, Karp's sign-variation theorem gives
$$
m(M)=4-\max_{|J|=2}\operatorname{var}
 \left(((-1)^i p_{J\cup\{i\}})_{i\notin J}\right),\tag{5}
$$
with each minor written in increasing index order and the five entries
ordered by $i$. The two computations agree on all 16,051,200 base
states. See Karp, [*Sign variation, the Grassmannian, and total positivity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.05622).

Finally, $m\le4$: choose a functional vanishing on $v_1,v_2$, then
perturb it so its values on $v_1,v_2$ have the sign of its nonzero value
on $v_3$. At most the four remaining adjacent changes can occur.

The extremal case also has an elementary sign characterization. After
normalizing $p_{567}>0$, one has $m=4$ if and only if
$\operatorname{sgn}p_{ijk}=(-1)^{i+j+k}$ for every ordered triple.
For the forward direction, fix $a<b$ and evaluate
$v\mapsto\det(v_a,v_b,v)$ on the other five columns. A small
perturbation fills its two zero entries without adding a sign change.
Since $m=4$, those five values must alternate. It follows that
$(-1)^{a+b+i}p_{\operatorname{sort}(a,b,i)}$ has a constant sign as
$i$ varies; connectedness of the graph of triples differing in one
element gives the same constant for every triple. The sign of $p_{567}$
fixes it to be positive. Conversely, if the minors have these alternating
signs and a strict row sign sequence had at most three sign changes, then
after multiplying its $i$-th sign by $(-1)^i$ it would have at least
three changes. Four entries could therefore be chosen to alternate; on
those four columns their original signs agree, up to a common sign, with
the coefficients of the four-column dependence. Applying the row
functional to that dependence gives a contradiction.

## 4. The verified rank and the proof

The most self-contained verifier requires **no data files**. It constructs
all 23,246,848 augmented states and 144,788,896 distinct directed sign-flip
edges, computes their strongly connected components, and constructs the
maximal future target-change rank $H$ on the resulting component graph.
It finds 783,708 components, no positive-weight cycle, and $H\le12$.
Then, independently of whether one trusts the component algorithm, it
checks directly that $0\le H(s)\le8+m(s)$ at every state and
$H(s)\ge H(t)+w$ at all 230,629,888 move-labeled edges, where $w=1$
for a target sign change and $w=0$ otherwise. These direct inequalities
alone prove the upper bound. An independent reverse-reachability audit also
confirms that $H$ is the exact optimal rank of the abstract graph.

A further certificate shows that the potential need only distinguish 41 of
the 52 signs: the 35 minors, four selected F signs including G67, and
G56 and G57. Its unfiltered projection has 4,960,256 distinct potential
states and 27,866,144 distinct projected sign-flip edges. It also has
maximum 12 and satisfies the sharper bound 8+m. A separate verifier checks
this projected potential against every one of the original 23,246,848
full states and all 230,629,888 move-labeled edges. The projection reduces
the size of the potential; the present construction and verification still
enumerate the full hidden sign space to establish these inequalities.

A second, independently checked certificate explains one route to the
same inequality. It starts from a byte array specifying an integer
$R\in\{0,\ldots,14\}$ for each 50-sign base state. The array was found
by a graph-rank construction; that construction need not be trusted. A
separate verifier reconstructs the states and moves and checks directly
that on every augmented edge $s\to t$,
$$
R(s)\ge R(t)+w,\qquad
R(s)\equiv\mathbf1_{\{p_{123}(s)<0\}}\pmod2.\tag{6}
$$

Call an edge *tight* if equality holds in (6), and let $C$ be the set of
all augmented states from which **no** directed tight path reaches a state
with $R\le1$. A zero-length path is allowed. The standalone program
reconstructs $C$ by reverse search: 7,806 low-rank seeds reach
12,391,562 states, leaving $|C|=10,855,286$.

Now define the corrected rank
$$
S(s)=R(s)-2\mathbf1_C(s).\tag{7}
$$
This is nonnegative because $C$ avoids $R\le1$. It is also a
prerank: $C$ is forward closed under tight edges, and the integer slack
in (6) is always even by the parity statement. Consequently any edge
leaving $C$ has slack at least two, exactly paying for the subtraction
in (7); the other cases are immediate.

The finite check further establishes $R\le10+m$ on every state and
places **all 1,751,988** states with $R>8+m$ in $C$. Therefore
$S\le8+m$. It also verifies the final statement directly, for **every**
admitted state and **every** candidate move-labeled edge:
$$
0\le S(s)\le8+m(s),\qquad S(s)\ge S(t)+w.\tag{8}
$$
Here $m(s)$ is the circuit-sign computation of Section 3. The maxima of
the two ranks in each variation class are:

| $m$ | Maximum $R$ | Maximum $S$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 10 | 8 |
| 1 | 11 | 9 |
| 2 | 12 | 10 |
| 3 | 13 | 11 |
| 4 | 14 | 12 |

Both standalone programs independently reconstruct all direction types and
base keys, derive the 114 active move rules by exact sparse polynomial
algebra, and check all 1,070 event-separation resultants. They use neither
the original graph source nor its event table or direction file. Separate
implementations independently check the canonical rank and an alternative
refined rank on all states and edges. The data-free program also passed a
combined address/undefined-behavior sanitizer run.

Both additional signs matter to this particular local graph construction.
If either is forgotten, even a projected model that requires one common
hidden sign on both sides of each visible event admits an abstract path
with 14 target changes. Those paths do not lift to the 52-sign graph under
an independently checked hidden-sign monitor. They are not real
14-crossing trajectories, and they do not rule out a different proof with
fewer tracked features.

As a separate diagnostic, 36,980 sign events generated along actual rational
trajectories, including 425 target crossings, satisfy state coverage,
variation monotonicity, and the earlier refined rank inequality. This
diagnostic does not replace the all-state proof check.

On a generic real trajectory, apply the second inequality in (8) at each
tracked sign event and telescope. Since the terminal value of $S$ is nonnegative,
the total number of target changes is at most the initial $S$, which is
at most $8+m(M)$. The perturbation argument in Section 2 extends the
result to every starting matrix with nonzero maximal minors and every
finite sequence with strict sampled target signs. This proves (1), subject
to the finite certificate and coverage audits described here. No parity
extension or separate halfspace theorem is needed for the refined bound.

The $m=0$ case of (1) also follows without the finite-state computation
from the known rank-two theorem. Choose $\omega$ positive on all seven
columns and write $P_i=v_i/\omega(v_i)$ and $e_i=P_{i+1}-P_i$.
All $\omega(v_i)$ remain positive under the allowed moves. An elementary
move at column $j$ replaces $P_j$ by a convex combination of
$P_j,P_{j+1}$; consequently, up to positive column scalings, it acts on
the six planar edge vectors by a positive elementary move. The sign of
$p_{123}$ is the sign of $\det(e_1,e_2)$. Thus the published
rank-two bound $r(2,6)=8$ gives $r(M)\le8$ in this class. A generic
perturbation handles projected degeneracies. The rank-two reduction is the
no-pole case of the construction in the cited finiteness paper.

The same projection gives a separate analytic bound for a larger class:
if a functional is positive on $v_2,\ldots,v_7$, then $r(M)\le9$,
or $r(M)\le8$ when $p_{123}$ and $p_{567}$ have the same sign.
Use the rank-two cyclic prerank $\rho$ of the six projected edge
vectors and add $\mathbf1_{\{\omega(v_1)<0\}}$. The only possible scalar
pole is at the first column; there the first projected direction reverses,
changing $\rho$ by exactly one in absolute value, while the indicator
falls by one and $p_{123}$ does not change. All other moves are positive
rank-two moves up to positive scalings. The detailed argument and parity
refinement are in `monotone_suffix_halfspace_prerank.md` in the audit
archive. This analytic argument does not cover every starting matrix.

One exact 105-move rational trajectory has all 52 tracked features nonzero
at all 106 recorded matrices and has 12 changes of $p_{123}$. Its
minimum row-space variation and remaining target changes at selected
starting vertices are:

| Start after move | $m$ | Later target changes |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 4 | 12 |
| 10 | 3 | 11 |
| 15 | 2 | 10 |
| 25 | 1 | 9 |
| 29 | 0 | 8 |

The 12 target changes occur at moves 10, 15, 25, 29, 37, 48, 63, 67,
78, 95, 102, and 105. Two independent exact replays use, respectively,
exterior-power updates and integer column arithmetic with direct
determinants. A separate earlier 207-move trajectory is also included; at
each of its 208 vertices, both the canonical rank $S$ and the exact
optimal rank of the abstract graph equal the number of target changes
remaining in that example. Separate short exact examples for all five
classes are included as well.

An optimal 28-move witness has an integer starting matrix and positive
integer move sizes. It changes the target sign at moves 1, 3, 5, 7, 10,
12, 15, 17, 21, 23, 26, and 28. Independent exact replays check that all
52 tracked features are nonzero at each recorded matrix. Its starting
matrix is

\begin{pmatrix}

0&75&-102&27&-8&1&0\\
0&0&-45&45&-180&60&-4
\end{pmatrix},
$$
and its sequence of pairs $(j,t)$, meaning $v_j\leftarrow v_j+t v_{j+1}$, is
$$
\begin{gathered}
(3,2),(2,4),(3,3),(4,2),(3,3),(2,132),(3,37),\\
(1,3),(2,3394),(3,435),(4,166),(3,213),(5,28),(4,368),\\

\end{gathered}
$$
The archive includes this integer witness and a separate, smaller rational
28-move witness, which is used for the 40-move construction below.

The certificate also implies a lower bound on the **number of
moves** needed for any 12-crossing example: at least 28. Twelve moves must
have label 3, and between successive target changes the derivative
$p_{124}$ must reverse sign, requiring at least 11 intervening moves
with labels 2 or 4. There must also be a move with label 1: without one,
column 1 is fixed and projection modulo that column gives a rank-two
problem with at most eight changes. Finally, labels 5 and 6 must each occur.
If label 6 is absent, append a generic seventh column close to the fixed
sixth column; a functional can then be chosen with variation at most 3,
contradicting (1) for 12 changes. If label 5 is absent, the first five
columns form a closed system and the same argument applies after deleting
label-6 moves. In fact, at least **two** label-5 moves are necessary. With
at most one, the portions before and after it each give a $(3,5)$ path,
with at most six changes each. If both had six, the first portion would
end with all its maximal minors of one sign, by the terminal-state theorem.
The label-5 move does not change $p_{123}$ or $p_{124}$, so these
two minors would still have the same sign afterward, precluding maximal
variation in the five-column system. Gale duality turns a $(3,5)$
positive-move sequence into a $(2,5)$ one; the published rank-two
prerank has maximum 6 only in the alternating chamber. The second portion
therefore has at most five changes. A separate 192-state check confirms
the sharper $(3,5)$ bound $r\le4+m_5$. The four required moves with labels
1, 5, 5, and 6 are distinct from the 23 already counted.

One more move is necessary. By (1), every 12-crossing start is alternating.
An independent finite check follows the ten signs of its first-five-column
minors through an over-approximation with 384 states. It permits a label-5
move to change those signs arbitrarily, subject only to preserving the four
minors on columns 1–4. It also allows a single label-1 move to generate an
arbitrary contiguous run of its sign events. Even with these relaxations,
at most 10 target changes are possible using at most two label-5 moves and
one label-1 move. Thus a 12-crossing sequence needs either a second label-1
move or a third label-5 move, giving at least 28 moves in all. Independent
implementations of this small check, including an exact event-separation
witness, are in `verify_minimum_28_independent.py` and
`verify_at_most_one_j1_two_j5.py`. The 28-move example attains this lower
bound, so the shortest possible witness has **exactly 28 moves**.

A finer six-column sign check eliminates one of the two possible
28-move allocations. It uses 23,808 necessary sign states, permits an
arbitrary label-6 jump preserving the first five columns' minor signs,
and still finds at most 11 target changes with at most one label-1 move,
three label-5 moves, and one label-6 move. An independent implementation
and a direct check of the computed rank inequalities agree. Consequently,
every 28-move witness must use exactly two label-1 moves, two label-5 moves,
one label-6 move, twelve label-3 moves, and eleven label-2 or label-4 moves.
The rational witness above provides a real realization of this profile.
Moreover, each of labels 2 and 4 occurs between four and seven times. Indeed,
consecutive label-2 separators need a label-1 move between them to reverse
$p_{134}$; consecutive label-4 separators similarly need a label-5 move
to reverse $p_{125}$. There are only two moves of each required kind.

The terminal matrix has all maximal minors positive, by the terminal-state
theorem: after 12 target changes no further target change is possible. The
unique label-6 move must flip $p_{456}$, which begins negative. Both
label-5 moves must flip $p_{345}$: this minor begins and ends positive,
only label 5 can change it, and a separate 384-state over-approximation
rules out 12 target changes if the two label-5 moves leave its sign fixed.
The check `verify_optimal_p345.py` explores 3,763 formal states and finds
at most 10 target changes under that assumption.

A more compact view of the same obstruction uses
$$
V=\operatorname{var}(p_{123},p_{124},p_{134},p_{234},p_{345}).
$$
The exact future rank of the 384-state first-five sign graph is either
$V$ or $V+2$ at every state, with 192 states of each kind. A
label-5 move preserving $p_{345}$ preserves all five signs in $V$,
so it can increase that graph rank by at most two; from initial rank six,
two such moves allow at most ten target changes. This rank formula is an
exhaustive finite result, not a claimed nonenumerative proof.

The two label-5 moves must also straddle the label-6 move. More generally,
any 12-crossing path with exactly one label-6 move must have a label-5
move both before and after it. If none follows, deleting the label-6 move
leaves the entire subsequent first-five-column trajectory unchanged. If
none precedes it, replace the initial sixth column by its value immediately
after that label-6 move and delete the move; the first five columns before
it do not depend on the sixth column, and the later first-five trajectory
is the same. In either case, perturb to make all recorded six-column
states generic, then append a fixed generic seventh column sufficiently
close to the sixth. This padded start has $m\le3$, contradicting (1).
Thus the order in every 28-move witness is $U_5,U_6,U_5$.
The two label-1 moves must have a label-2 move between them: otherwise they
commute past the intervening moves, none of which changes column 2, and
merge into one positive label-1 move. Since label 1 leaves the target minor
unchanged, a small generic perturbation would then give a forbidden
27-move, 12-crossing path.

There is a useful description of the extremal starting chamber. The exact
graph has only 12 augmented states with optimal rank 12. Every one has the
alternating minor signs $\operatorname{sgn}p_{ijk}=(-1)^{i+j+k}$, and
$$
G_{56}<0,\qquad G_{57}>0,\qquad G_{67}<0.
$$
The other 24 admitted formal states with $m=4$ have optimal graph rank
10, but they are not realizable: the G signs displayed above are in fact
forced by the alternating minor signs. In that chamber the three ratios
$$
r_i=\frac{p_{12i}}{p_{34i}},\qquad i=5,6,7,
$$
are automatically strictly ordered:
$$
r_5>r_6>r_7>0.
$$
To see this, reorient columns alternately to get a matrix with all
maximal minors $\Delta_{ijk}>0$; the ratios $r_i$ are unchanged.
For $4<i<j$, the Plücker identity
$$
\Delta_{14i}(\Delta_{12i}\Delta_{34j}
 -\Delta_{12j}\Delta_{34i})
=\Delta_{12i}\Delta_{134}\Delta_{4ij}
 +\Delta_{124}\Delta_{1ij}\Delta_{34i}
$$
has strictly positive terms on the right, so $r_i>r_j$. Thus the
ratio order is **not** an additional real restriction beyond $m=4$.

Each of the 12 rank-12 sign states is nevertheless realized by a moment-
curve starting matrix for which an exact rational positive-move sequence
achieves 12 changes. Independent exterior-power replays check all 35
maximal minors at each sampled matrix. Strictness shows that each sign
chamber contains an open set of such sharp starts. The following independent
positive-cell argument strengthens existence to **every** generic
$m=4$ starting matrix.
One especially simple start is
$$
v_i=(-1)^i(1,i,i^2),\qquad i=1,\ldots,7.
$$
An exact **28-move** continuation from these columns has 12 target changes;
the data and two independent rational replays are included in the archive.
For these exact columns, every 28-move, 12-crossing path with all maximal
minors nonzero at recorded vertices must start with a label-3 move of size
$t$ satisfying $1/3<t<2/3$. The lower bound holds because a shortest
path must flip the target on its first move and $p_{123}=2-6t$. At
$t=2/3$, $p_{237}=0$, violating the strict-vertex condition. For
$t>2/3$, choose
$$
\omega_\epsilon=(14-3\epsilon,-9+\epsilon,1),\qquad
0<\epsilon<\min\{1/2,(6t-4)/(2t)\}.
$$
Its signs on the seven columns after the first move are $-,-,-,-,+,-,-$,
so the new state has $m\le2$ and allows at most 10 further target changes
by (1). Since the target is then negative, the parity refinement improves
this to at most nine further changes, hence at most 10 in total. Thus no
strictly generic 28-move path from these columns has
all move sizes positive integers. This does not rule out integer move sizes
if incidental zero minors are allowed at recorded vertices. The upper
restriction above applies to any strict 12-change path whose first move is
$U_3(t)$; the lower restriction requires that first move to flip the
target, as it must in a 28-move path.
For comparison, the uniform flow from this same unscaled start has no
positive target zeroes, so the successful continuation uses nonuniform
positive controls.

Let $D=\operatorname{diag}((-1)^1,\ldots,(-1)^7)$, let $W$ be the
fixed 28-move witness, and let $X=\operatorname{row}(MD)$. Since
$m(M)=4$, $X$ is in the positive Grassmannian. With
$E=[0\mid I_3]$, set
$$
T(t)=U_4(t_1)U_3(t_2)U_2(t_3)U_1(t_4)
 U_5(t_5)U_4(t_6)U_3(t_7)U_2(t_8)
 U_6(t_9)U_5(t_{10})U_4(t_{11})U_3(t_{12}).
$$
Every positive 3-plane $X$ has a representation $X=\operatorname{row}(ET(t^0))$
with all $t_i^0>0$, and this 12-parameter chart has an invertible
derivative. A direct symbolic check verifies explicit rational inverse
formulas: after normalizing $X=[A\mid I_3]$, with its ordered minors
denoted by $\Delta$, they are
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
t_1=\dfrac{\Delta_{167}\Delta_{456}}{\Delta_{156}},&
t_2=\dfrac{\Delta_{156}\Delta_{345}}{\Delta_{145}\Delta_{456}},\$$2mm]
t_3=\dfrac{\Delta_{145}\Delta_{234}}{\Delta_{134}\Delta_{345}},&
t_4=\dfrac{\Delta_{134}}{\Delta_{234}},\$$2mm]
t_5=\dfrac{\Delta_{127}\Delta_{156}}{\Delta_{126}\Delta_{167}},&
t_6=\dfrac{\Delta_{126}\Delta_{145}}{\Delta_{125}\Delta_{156}},\$$2mm]
t_7=\dfrac{\Delta_{125}\Delta_{134}}{\Delta_{124}\Delta_{145}},&
t_8=\dfrac{\Delta_{124}}{\Delta_{134}},\$$2mm]
t_9=\dfrac{\Delta_{126}}{\Delta_{127}},&
t_{10}=\dfrac{\Delta_{125}}{\Delta_{126}},\$$2mm]
t_{11}=\dfrac{\Delta_{124}}{\Delta_{125}},&
t_{12}=\dfrac{\Delta_{123}}{\Delta_{124}}.
\end{array}\tag{9}
$$
All are positive. Substituting them into the normalized product $ET(t)$
recovers $X$, so the square derivative is invertible.

Now put $\Lambda_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon,
\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\varepsilon,1,1,1)$ and
$Y_\varepsilon=\operatorname{row}(WD\Lambda_\varepsilon)$. As
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$, this plane tends to $E$. The implicit
function theorem gives positive $t(\varepsilon)$ with
$Y_\varepsilon T(t(\varepsilon))=X$ for sufficiently small positive
$\varepsilon$. Reversing the factors and conjugating by $D$ turns
$T^{-1}$ into 12 positive shears, taking $M$ to
$A W\Lambda_\varepsilon$ for some $A\in GL_3$. Every intermediate
prefix remains in the alternating chamber: after reorientation it is
$Y_\varepsilon$ followed by a shorter positive-shear product, and
such products preserve positivity of all maximal minors. In particular,
the prefix has no target change and all its vertices are generic.

Positive column scaling transfers each witness shear $U_j(t)$ to
$U_j(t\lambda_j/\lambda_{j+1})$. Thus the fixed 28-move witness
provides 12 changes after the 12-move prefix, for a total of 40 moves.
For rational starting matrices, nearby positive rational parameters
preserve all the strict signs and give a rational sequence. The explicit
chart identities and a separate elimination audit are included in the
archive.

The archive also contains an exact, terminating constructor for a rational
start. It computes the first three Taylor coefficients of the implicit
12-parameter solution over the rationals, tries a sequence of positive
rational $\varepsilon$'s tending to zero, and accepts only after exact
checks of all maximal-minor signs in the preparation and witness. The
Taylor error is $O(\varepsilon^4)$, whereas positivity and Cauchy–Binet
give a lower bound of order $\varepsilon^3$ for each ideal preparation
minor. After undoing the positive column scaling, the final error tends to
zero as well. Thus the search terminates; it does not rely on numerical
root finding or on the finite-state upper-bound computation. No uniform
running-time bound is claimed.

There is also an independent elementary construction of the first two
changes:
from **every** generic $m=4$ start, three positive moves with labels
$3,4,3$ can be chosen to produce two target changes and end at a matrix
with $m=2$. After alternating reorientation, the first shear reaches
$\Delta_{123}=0$ before any other maximal-minor wall; the next two
shears can be chosen by a two-scale perturbation. Plücker identities
control the intermediate wall signs, and two four-column circuits give
the final $m=2$ lower bound. A complete elementary proof and a rational
example are included as `universal_first_two_flips.md` and its verifier.
The construction does not yet supply the ten remaining target changes
from every resulting matrix.

The first-wall part extends to all $0<k<n$: from a generic start of
maximum possible minimum row variation $m=n-k$, a suitable positive move
at column $k$ crosses the target before any other maximal-minor wall,
and the new matrix has $m=n-k-1$. This follows from the total-positive
Plücker ratio inequalities and the sign-variation formula; the detailed
argument is in `universal_first_wall_all_k_n.md` in the audit archive.

Independently, every such start admits four target changes before entering
the $m=0$ class. Keep $v_1,v_2$ fixed and let
$x_i=p_{12i}$ for $i=3,\ldots,7$; their signs initially alternate
$+,-,+,-,+$. Use move labels
$$
3,\ 4,3,\ 5,4,3,\ 6,5,4,3,
$$
each time choosing the positive parameter so the moved $x_j$ crosses
zero toward the sign of $x_{j+1}$. The four moves labeled 3 give four
target changes; afterwards $x_3,\ldots,x_7>0$. The functional
$v\mapsto\det(v_1,v_2,v)$ can then be perturbed to be positive on all
seven columns, so $m=0$. Generic parameters avoid unwanted zero minors
at the sampled vertices. This simple cascade does **not** guarantee the
eight remaining changes: an explicit exact example of the cascade ends at
an abstract graph state of rank 6, allowing at most six further changes.

## 6. An elementary terminal-state theorem in all dimensions

For $0<k<n$ and any generic real $k\times n$ matrix, normalize the last maximal
minor to be positive. Then the target minor has no future sign change
under positive elementary moves **if and only if all maximal minors are
positive**. More precisely, a negative initial target admits at least one
future change; a positive target together with any negative maximal minor
admits at least two. This statement is independent of the finite graph.

The key observation is that positive moves can make the target acquire the
initial sign of any chosen maximal minor $p_I$. Let
$U_j(t)=I+tE_{j+1,j}$,
$B=(U_{n-1}(1)\cdots U_1(1))^k$, and let the diagonal entries of $D_L$
be $L$ on $I$ and 1 elsewhere. Every flag minor
$\Delta_{J,[k]}(B)$ is strictly positive; in fact it is a Vandermonde
product divided by $\prod_{r=0}^{k-1}r!$. Cauchy–Binet gives
$$
\Bigl(\prod_{c=1}^{k}d_c\Bigr)
 p_{[k]}(MD_LBD_L^{-1})
 =\sum_{|J|=k}p_J(M)\Delta_{J,[k]}(B)L^{|J\cap I|}.
$$
The unique leading term is $p_I(M)\Delta_{I,[k]}(B)L^k$, so a
sufficiently large $L$ selects the sign of $p_I(M)$. The conjugated
matrix is exactly a product of $k(n-1)$ positive shears. Generic
intermediate vertices are obtained by a small parameter perturbation.

If the initial target is negative, select the positive last minor. If it is
positive and some minor is negative, first select a negative minor, then
select the still-positive last minor. Conversely, positive moves preserve
positivity of every maximal minor. The full derivation, including an
explicit choice of $L$, is in `zero_future_flips_all_k_n.md` in the
accompanying audit archive.

A second general structural consequence concerns **maximum-variation**
starts. An elementary positive-Grassmannian chart extends the
positive-cell transfer above to all $k,n$: any two generic
starts with $m=n-k$ have the same future rank. In addition, any generic
matrix is reachable from some maximum-variation start by a finite positive
word. Therefore every maximum-variation start has the global future rank,
whatever that rank is. In particular, the known lower bound $k(n-k)$ is
attained from every such start. This observation does not give the
conjectured upper bound in general. A proof, including its use of the
explicit chart, its rational inverse, and the backward-extension argument,
is in `elementary_positive_chart_and_global_rank.md` in the audit archive.

There is also an elementary classification of starts with at most one
future target change, valid in every dimension. Order $k$-subsets
componentwise and normalize the last minor positive. Then
$$
r(M)\le1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{I:p_I(M)<0\}\text{ is a lower order ideal}.\tag{10}
$$
When the ideal condition holds, the rank is 0 or 1 according to whether
the target starts positive or negative. If it fails, the rank is at least
2 for a positive target and at least 3 for a negative target. The proof
uses the short Plücker relation to show that the ideal condition is
preserved by positive shears; conversely, a diagonal scaling makes a
positive lower minor dominate the target while retaining a negative upper
minor. The complete proof is in `rank_one_ideal_theorem.md`. Combined with
an exhaustive tally of the finite graph, it also shows that the graph
rank $H$ is the actual rank on every realizable strict state with
$H\le3$; no such exactness claim is made for larger $H$.

A second sign-only lower bound works in every dimension. For each
$(k-1)$-subset $S$, list its complement in increasing order. Then
$$
r(M)\ge\operatorname{var}\Bigl(p_{[k]},\
  (p_{S\cup\{i\}})_{i\notin S},\ p_{\{n-k+1,\ldots,n\}}\Bigr).\tag{11}
$$
One can maximize this expression over $S$. Successive positive words,
obtained by diagonal conjugation, make the target take the sign of each
listed pencil minor in order while preserving the signs of the later ones.
The proof and an exhaustive comparison with the $(3,7)$ graph are in
`pencil_variation_lower_bound.md`.

## 7. Reproducing the checks

The accompanying archive contains the data-free source, the alternative
rank data, exact examples, and a `run_all.sh` script. It requires Python
3.10 or newer with SymPy and a GCC/Clang-compatible C++17 compiler; the
graph verifiers use multiple threads and substantial memory. Run
`./run_all.sh` from the extracted archive. The
script checks the symbolic identities, circuit masks, independently
reconstructed states, generic-event resultants, the ranks, and the sharp
rational examples. The data-free program also runs by itself with a C++17
compiler, without Python or an input file. The smaller archive also offers
`./run_low_memory.sh`, which reads bundled rank data and verifies all the
inequalities directly, using approximately 565 MiB on the reference run.

array is

The additional arrays in the archive support the independent historical
checks; the standalone proof does not use them. Assertions must remain
enabled when compiling the C++ verifiers. The primary data-free source with
